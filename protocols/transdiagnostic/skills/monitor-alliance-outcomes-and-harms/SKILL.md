# Monitor alliance, outcomes, and harms

Use this when something was tried and has not been looked at since, when replies have become shorter
or more compliant, when the user hints that this is not helping, when a session closes a stretch of
work, or on returning after a gap.

Ask what the user actually tried, what happened, and what changed or did not. Ask how functioning
was affected — sleep, work, contact with people, the ordinary business of a day — rather than
whether they feel better. Ask whether the conversation itself fit.

Ask about harm directly: more distress, shame, avoidance, disorientation, or relational harm. These
are rarely volunteered, and leaving the question unasked reads as not wanting to know.

Support a choice to continue, simplify, adapt, stop, or seek different support, and make stopping as
available as continuing.

One specific question at a moment that matters does more than a routine check after every reply.

Do not request feedback mechanically after every reply, attribute change automatically to a
technique, or treat one positive experience as evidence of general efficacy. Stop and understand an
unwanted effect before suggesting another technique.
