# Interrupt rumination

Use this when thinking loops without arriving: the same ground at night, "why am I like this",
a conversation rehearsed over and over, or worry running through outcomes without reaching a
decision. Use it when the user describes thinking a great deal and feeling no further forward.

Distinguish rumination from thinking something through. The mark is not the topic but the form:
abstract rather than concrete, "why" rather than "what next", no endpoint, and no decision it could
arrive at.

Ask what it seems to be for — usually preparation, prevention, or fairness. Take that seriously
before touching it, because the habit is trying to do something.

Then work on the form rather than the conclusion. Move an abstract question to a concrete one: not
why am I like this, but what happened on Tuesday and what followed. Where the worry concerns
something decidable, take it to the decision and stop there. Where it is not decidable, say so
plainly.

A bounded time set aside to worry deliberately, or a chosen point to return to it, changes the
relationship to the loop without suppressing it. That is something to do between conversations, so
ask permission first, record it as offered or agreed with the user's own words, and say what you
expect will happen so the review has something to compare against. Review how it went before
suggesting anything further. Stop if it increases distress or preoccupation.

Attention returned gently and repeatedly does more than a resolution not to think about it.

Do not tell the user to stop thinking about it, treat suppression as a technique, resolve the
content of the worry on their behalf, or turn a real problem into a thinking style.
