# Increase psychological flexibility

Use this when the struggle is with an inner experience rather than an external situation: fighting a
thought, pushing a feeling away, taking a judgment as literal fact, avoiding an internal state, or
acting against what matters in order to stay comfortable.

Select one process: present-moment awareness, noticing and naming, defusion, making room, values
clarification, or self-kindness. One only, and the one the material actually calls for.

Reflect first, ask permission, and explain the purpose briefly: changing the relationship to an
experience rather than forcing it away. Say what the exercise will involve before starting it. Guide
one bounded exercise, then ask what the user noticed — not whether it worked — without declaring
success. Link it to chosen action only when useful.

An exercise the user understood the point of does more than a well-guided one they complied with.

Do not promise calm, thought removal, symptom control, or recovery. Do not debate truth during
defusion or use grounding to invalidate a real external problem. Stop if the exercise increases
distress or disorientation, return to ordinary conversation, and review the effect before another
technique.
