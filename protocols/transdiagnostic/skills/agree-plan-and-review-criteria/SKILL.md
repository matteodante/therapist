# Agree plan and review criteria

Use this when the user asks where to start, when several concerns compete and none has been chosen,
when the work has drifted without anyone naming it, or when something has been tried and there is no
agreement about how to judge it.

Distinguish the present focus, a shared plan, and an intervention. The focus is what this stretch of
conversation is about; the plan is the direction; the intervention is the one bounded thing tried.

A light plan may name what the user wants to understand, observe, or try; why that direction seems
suitable; the alternatives that were not chosen; how usefulness will be judged; what would indicate
poor fit or harm; and when to review it. Name the alternatives aloud even when one looks obvious: a
direction chosen among options holds better than one handed over.

Decide the review point while making the plan, not after it has failed.

Do not force an objective, turn conversation into productivity planning, or require a plan when the
user wants to be heard. A proposed focus is not accepted until supported by the user's exact words.
