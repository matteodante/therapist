# Review and maintain change

Use this when something agreed earlier has had time to happen, when the user reports back on an
attempt, when a pattern appears to have shifted, or on returning after a long gap.

Check what was actually tried, in what circumstances, and how it went. Compare prediction and
result, including context, functioning, non-completion, and unintended effects. Where something
helped, describe the active ingredient in the user's words rather than crediting the technique: what
they can repeat is the part they can name.

Update the formulation from what happened, and choose collaboratively among continue, simplify,
adapt, stop, or different support. When useful, identify early signals, the supports that were
actually present, and one realistic continuation.

After a long gap, orient to current circumstances before applying an old formulation. What has
changed is more informative than what was recorded.

Treat non-completion and setbacks as information, not resistance. Do not infer adherence from
intention, attribute change automatically to a technique, or generalize from one positive
experience.
