# Primary-source basis for five therapeutic skills

Source check: 4 August 2026. This note maps primary or first-party public-health sources to five
independently authored Therapist skills that lacked `references/basis.md` at the start of this
research pass. It does not
copy an intervention, establish clinical validation, or support autonomous AI treatment.

## Conclusion

Each skill has a genuine source basis suitable for an original, narrow `basis.md`. The strongest
matches are the WHO/UNICEF helping-skills manual for `stay-with-emotion`, NHS and NICE cognitive
material for `examine-a-thought`, NHS/WA Health plus a primary trial for `interrupt-rumination`, and
WHO Step-by-Step, WHO PM+, and NICE for `restore-activity`.

`meet-self-criticism` is supportable through WHO self-kindness and WHO/UNICEF validation guidance,
with the Western Australia public mental-health service as a more specific first-party source. One
sentence in the skill needs particular epistemic care: the claim that criticism is “usually old and
was once useful” is not established by the selected sources. Asking whether it developed earlier or
served a protective function can remain a tentative question, but the source record should not
present that explanation as a general fact.

## Source register

### S1 — WHO/UNICEF foundational helping skills

- World Health Organization and UNICEF, *Foundational helping skills training manual: a
  competency-based approach for training helpers to support adults*.
- Publication date: 10 July 2025. ISBN 978-92-4-010593-5. The current English artifact contains a
  corrigendum dated 25 August 2025.
- Official record:
  https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240105935
- Current English artifact:
  https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/381678/9789240105935-eng.pdf
- Relevant content: close, non-judgmental listening; reflective statements; tentative naming of
  emotions that the person can correct; asking about feelings and bodily experience; validation and
  normalization without trivializing the individual experience.
- Limits: this is a competency-based training manual for helpers, trainers, and supervisors. It
  includes training, role-play, assessment, and supervision assumptions; it does not validate an
  unsupervised conversational agent.

### S2 — WHO Doing What Matters

- World Health Organization, *Doing What Matters in Times of Stress: An Illustrated Guide*.
- Publication date: 29 April 2020. ISBN 978-92-4-000392-7.
- Official record:
  https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927
- Relevant content: noticing and naming difficult thoughts and feelings; “making room” rather than
  fighting or suppressing them; curious attention to bodily sensations; noticing and unhooking from
  harsh self-judgments; responding to one's own pain with kindness.
- Limits: general stress/adversity self-help, not diagnosis or treatment for every condition. The
  publication is under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO; a citation does not authorize copying its scripts or
  illustrations into Therapist.

### S3 — Western Australia public mental-health self-compassion resources

- Centre for Clinical Interventions (CCI), *Self Compassion* and *Building Self-Compassion: From
  Self-Criticism to Self-Kindness*.
- Landing-page revision: last updated 5 September 2019.
- Official resource:
  https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Self-Compassion
- First-party status: CCI is a specialist statewide programme in the Western Australia public mental
  health system:
  https://www.health.wa.gov.au/sitecore/content/Healthy-WA/Articles/A_E/Centre-for-Clinical-Interventions
- Relevant content: self-criticism as a response that can sustain threat and distress; awareness of
  self-critical thinking; beliefs that can make self-compassion difficult; relating to one's own pain
  with care rather than escalating self-attack.
- Limits: the workbook is condition-oriented self-help information and recommends professional
  support where needed. The public page does not provide a distinct edition number for the workbook;
  if referenced in a manifest, use the landing-page revision rather than inventing one.

### S4 — NHS thought record

- NHS Every Mind Matters, *Thought record*.
- Revision: current page; no publication or revision date is exposed on the page. Verified
  4 August 2026.
- Official resource:
  https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/thought-record/
- Relevant content: start from one concrete situation; identify the thought and feelings; examine
  factual evidence for and against the thought; consider other realistic or neutral readings; review
  the resulting change.
- Limits: the NHS exercise proceeds to an alternative thought. Therapist's skill is deliberately
  narrower and more collaborative: it does not supply a preferred conclusion and ends with the
  user's own current view. The NHS page is public self-help information, not evidence that every
  distressing or danger-related thought should be challenged.

### S5 — NICE NG222

- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, NG222, *Depression in adults: treatment and
  management*.
- Published 29 June 2022; last reviewed 30 January 2026, with no recommendation update.
- Overview:
  https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222
- Recommendations:
  https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222/chapter/Recommendations
- Relevant content: CBT focuses on interaction among thoughts, beliefs, attitudes, feelings, and
  behaviour and on current issues; behavioural activation identifies activity–mood links, avoidance,
  withdrawal, inactivity, and practical behavioural change; guided self-help requires support and
  review of progress and outcomes.
- Limits: adult-depression guidance for health and care practice, with trained-practitioner and
  service assumptions. It is not unrestricted transdiagnostic guidance and does not validate AI
  delivery. NICE reuse and geographical/product restrictions remain applicable.

### S6 — NHS worry guidance

- NHS Every Mind Matters, *Tackling your worries*.
- Revision: current page; no publication or revision date is exposed on the page. Verified
  4 August 2026.
- Official resource:
  https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/tackling-your-worries/
- Relevant content: a bounded “worry time”; writing concerns down; returning attention to the
  present; distinguishing actionable problems from hypothetical worries outside one's control;
  planning a specific response to actionable concerns.
- Limits: public worry-management guidance, not a full account of depressive rumination and not a
  basis for suppressing thoughts. The page explicitly says worry cannot be eliminated completely.

### S7 — Western Australia worry and rumination resources

- Centre for Clinical Interventions, *Worry and Rumination* and *What? Me Worry!?!*.
- Landing-page revision: last updated 10 July 2023.
- Official resource:
  https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/resources/looking-after-yourself/worry-and-rumination
- Relevant content: worry versus effective problem-solving; positive beliefs that worry prepares,
  prevents, or produces solutions; attention training; worry postponement; discrimination between a
  current controllable problem and a hypothetical or uncontrollable concern.
- Limits: much of the workbook is framed around chronic worry and generalized anxiety disorder. It
  cannot by itself support a transdiagnostic claim about every form of rumination.

### S8 — primary concreteness-training trial

- Watkins ER, Taylor RS, Byng R, Baeyens C, Read R, Pearson K, Watson L, *Guided self-help
  concreteness training as an intervention for major depression in primary care: a Phase II
  randomized controlled trial*.
- Published online 16 November 2011; journal issue July 2012, *Psychological Medicine* 42(7),
  1359–1371. PMID 22085757; PMCID PMC3359637.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291711002480
- Full primary paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3359637/
- Relevant content: the trial operationalized a move from abstract, overgeneral processing to more
  concrete, specific processing and reported reductions in rumination relative to treatment as
  usual; the advantage on depressive symptoms was not significant against the active relaxation
  control.
- Limits: Phase II, adults with major depression in primary care, repeated guided self-help training,
  and not a test of Therapist's single conversational move. It supports the abstract-to-concrete
  distinction only with those qualifications. The PMC article is CC BY-NC-SA 2.5; link and summarize
  rather than copying the intervention.

### S9 — WHO Step-by-Step

- World Health Organization, *Psychological self-help interventions: web annex: Step-by-Step: a
  self-help intervention for depression*.
- Publication date: 1 June 2026. WHO reference B09738.
- Official record:
  https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/B09738
- Relevant content: the activity–mood cycle; restarting small activities that were previously
  enjoyed or mattered; breaking activities into manageable steps; planning and observing what
  happens afterward; self-kindness and social connection alongside activity.
- Limits: depression-specific self-help, tested in implementations that included brief regular
  support from trained non-specialist helpers. The source includes complete intervention content;
  Therapist should retain only an independently written minimal abstraction and not copy scripts,
  storylines, exercises, or illustrations.

### S10 — WHO Problem Management Plus

- World Health Organization, *Problem Management Plus (PM+): individual psychological help for
  adults impaired by distress in communities exposed to adversity*, generic field-trial version 1.1.
- Publication date: 1 January 2018. WHO reference WHO/MSD/MER/18.5.
- Official record:
  https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-MSD-MER-18.5
- Relevant content: the “Get Going, Keep Doing” chapter describes the low-mood/inactivity cycle,
  pleasant and task-oriented activities, action before waiting for motivation, very small goals,
  breaking tasks into steps, choosing a day and time, and reviewing obstacles and outcomes.
- Limits: PM+ is a complete intervention for adults impaired by distress in adversity-affected
  communities. It assumes helper training, competency, supervision, risk procedures, and contextual
  adaptation. WHO's non-commercial ShareAlike terms apply; do not copy its scripts or algorithm.

## Content discrepancies to resolve or qualify

The sources are adequate for citation, but they do not turn every sentence in the current skills
into an evidence claim:

- `stay-with-emotion`: S1 supports tentative emotion naming and correction, but not a general rule
  that one emotion “covers” another or that accuracy always validates more than sympathy. Those are
  editorial hypotheses and comparisons, not findings established by S1/S2.
- `meet-self-criticism`: S1 supports validation before further exploration, but does not establish
  that early disagreement proves disclosure was unsafe. S3 supports asking what self-criticism may
  be trying to achieve or prevent and what it costs; it does not show that criticism is generally old
  or was genuinely useful.
- `examine-a-thought`: “I am bad at this” is a broad evaluative thought, not a mood. A technically
  accurate operational distinction would be that it is too broad to examine usefully until tied to a
  concrete moment. S4 supports evidence review but also proceeds to reframing, which Therapist
  deliberately narrows.
- `interrupt-rumination`: preparation and prevention are documented positive beliefs about worry;
  “fairness” was not supported by the checked sources. Abstract-versus-concrete processing is a
  qualified research finding, not a universal diagnostic test for rumination.
- `restore-activity`: S10 directly supports action before waiting for motivation, while S9 and S5
  support small action, activity–mood links, and review. The current skill phrase is a concise
  abstraction, not wording to attribute verbatim to WHO. None of the sources supports treating
  illness or genuine exhaustion as avoidance.

## Reuse and evidence boundary

Use links and original summaries only. Do not copy WHO scripts, illustrations, exercises, or
storylines; NHS/CCI worksheets or phrasing; NICE recommendation wording; or the paper's intervention
protocol. The five skills remain experimental conversation abstractions. Their source basis does not
show safety or effectiveness for Therapist, does not create a clinical claim, and does not remove the
need for the planned live-model and bilingual safety evaluations.
